## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** RAG + Structured Extraction, with a mandatory Human-in-the-loop approval gate

**Description:**
When a tenant receives an inbound security questionnaire (the inverse of the existing outbound `questionnaire_service.py` flow), this system retrieves relevant tenant evidence/control data via the existing `rag_service.py` (ChromaDB), drafts a grounded answer per question via `ai_service.generate_text()`, and holds every draft in a "Pending Review" state — mirroring the Phase 15 evidence-review workflow shape — until a human explicitly approves it. Nothing is ever auto-submitted. "Good" means: every drafted answer is traceable to specific retrieved evidence (no ungrounded claims), and a human always makes the final submission call.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. A drafted answer states something not actually supported by retrieved evidence (hallucinated claim) and a reviewer approves it without noticing, because the source evidence wasn't shown alongside the draft.
2. A draft answer is submitted (marked "Submitted") without ever passing through the mandatory human review/approval step — RAG-02's core safety requirement silently bypassed by a code path.
3. Retrieval pulls evidence from the wrong tenant (cross-tenant leak) because `rag_service.py`'s ChromaDB collection isn't correctly tenant-scoped when queried for this new use case.
4. A low-confidence or no-evidence-found case is drafted anyway with confident-sounding text instead of being flagged to the reviewer as "insufficient evidence."

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** GRC / Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM) — specifically the "receiving side" of vendor security due diligence: responding to inbound SIG/CAIQ/VSA-style or custom security questionnaires sent by a customer, partner, or auditor. This is the inverse of the platform's existing outbound `questionnaire_service.py` flow (which sends questionnaires to *this tenant's own vendors*).

**User Population:** Internal Compliance/GRC/Security team members at the tenant company (the platform's customer) who are responsible for drafting and approving answers to security questionnaires their own customers or auditors send them — typically a Compliance Manager or GRC Analyst as primary drafter/reviewer, with Security Engineers pulled in for technically specific claims.

**Stakes Level:** Critical

**Output Consequence:** A reviewed-and-approved draft becomes the tenant's submitted answer to an external customer, partner, or auditor. Per practitioner research, security questionnaire answers are frequently incorporated by reference into the resulting commercial contract and function as contractual representations — an inaccurate answer is a potential breach-of-contract or misrepresentation exposure, not merely a reputational one. The drafted answer can also directly gate whether a sales deal closes (procurement teams routinely block deals on unsatisfactory security answers) or whether a partner/auditor accepts the tenant's risk profile.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

```
Dimension: Evidence traceability (grounding)
Good: Every claim in the answer text is traceable to a specific, named evidence artifact (policy doc, control ID, SOC 2 report section, ticket, scan result) a reviewer can click through and verify actually supports that exact claim.
Bad: The answer makes a security-practice claim (e.g., "we encrypt all data at rest") with no cited evidence, or cites evidence that is real but doesn't actually support the specific claim made.
Stakes: Critical
Source: Practitioner GRC workflow — evidence-linking and auditability are standing top-tier buyer criteria for 2026 questionnaire-automation tooling (Wolfia, Compyl)

Dimension: Present-tense certainty calibration (no overclaiming)
Good: Answer states current, verified practice ("MFA is enforced org-wide via Okta, verified by evidence pulled Sept 2026") and explicitly flags partial or planned controls as such ("MFA is enforced for admin accounts; end-user rollout is in progress, target Q4").
Bad: Answer asserts a control is fully implemented when the cited evidence shows only partial, planned, or stale coverage — "optimistic" phrasing that describes intended practice as current practice.
Stakes: Critical
Source: Practitioner research — describing security practices "as they are intended to work rather than as they actually function" is documented as the most common real-world questionnaire-response failure, more common than outright fabrication (Targhee Security, Eskasecurity)

Dimension: Scope and boundary precision
Good: Answer states exactly what the control covers — which product, environment, data classification, or region — matching the scope of the inbound question.
Bad: A control evidenced for one environment (e.g., production AWS only) is stated as if it applies tenant-wide, or a scoped question ("does this apply to your EU processing?") gets an unscoped, blanket answer.
Stakes: High
Source: GRC review practice — scope mismatches are a recurring, well-documented cause of questionnaire answers being contested at audit or breach-postmortem time

Dimension: "Insufficient evidence" honesty (no silent gap-filling)
Good: When no matching evidence exists for a question, the draft explicitly states insufficient evidence and is routed to the reviewer as a gap requiring manual research — never filled with plausible-sounding generic security language.
Bad: A no-evidence question receives a confident, generic best-practice-sounding answer that reads as tenant-specific but isn't actually grounded in this tenant's real controls.
Stakes: Critical
Source: Practitioner guidance — "never guess" is the standing rule for human questionnaire responders; mirrors Critical Failure Mode #4 in Section 1

Dimension: Reviewability of the draft-to-approval trail
Good: Every submitted answer has a retrievable record of what was originally drafted, which evidence was cited, who reviewed it, what (if anything) they edited, and when it was approved.
Bad: An answer reaches "submitted" status with no reviewer identity attached, no diff between draft and final text, or no way to reconstruct after the fact whether a human actually read it before approving.
Stakes: High
Source: GRC buyer evaluation criteria — "auditability ensures every response has a clear history... who drafted it, who reviewed it, and when it was approved" (Wolfia, 2026 GRC tooling criteria)
```

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Optimistic answer drift** — the draft describes a control as fully operational when the cited evidence only supports a partial or planned state. Documented as the most common real-world questionnaire-response failure — driven by unfamiliarity and a tendency toward optimistic phrasing, not deliberate dishonesty.
2. **Scope creep in answers** — a control evidenced for one environment or product line gets stated as if it applies organization-wide, misrepresenting the tenant's actual coverage to the counterparty asking.
3. **Stale evidence presented as current** — the draft cites evidence that is technically real but outdated (e.g., a pentest report or access review from over a year ago) without flagging staleness. The platform already treats staleness as first-class risk elsewhere (`STALE-01`/`STALE-02`); a questionnaire draft that ignores that same staleness signal when citing evidence undermines that existing product guarantee.
4. **Silent gap-filling** — when no evidence matches a question, the model reaches for generic industry-standard-sounding language instead of surfacing "insufficient evidence," producing an answer that *reads* tenant-specific but is actually fabricated boilerplate a reviewer may not catch without cross-referencing every claim manually.
5. **Reviewer rubber-stamping** — because source evidence isn't shown side-by-side with the draft (or a reviewer is under deadline pressure to turn around a 100+ question SIG), a plausible-sounding but ungrounded or overclaimed answer gets approved without the evidence actually being checked. This is Critical Failure Mode #1 from Section 1, restated as a human-factor behavior — reviewer fatigue on long questionnaires is a well-documented real-world GRC pattern, not just a hypothetical system-design gap.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

Drafting an answer is not itself a directly regulated activity (no HIPAA/GDPR trigger from the act of generating text), but the *substance* of the answers routinely makes representations about SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, and HIPAA controls — so the drafted content inherits whatever regulatory claims it asserts on the tenant's behalf. The primary real-world exposure is contractual: per practitioner research, questionnaire answers are frequently incorporated by reference into the resulting customer contract and are treated as evidence in any subsequent contractual or regulatory dispute if a later breach contradicts what was submitted. This is the concrete reason RAG-02's mandatory human-approval gate is a compliance control, not a UX nicety — it is the tenant's last checkpoint before generating a document with contractual weight. No new regulatory framework is triggered by building this feature; the constraint is that the system must not weaken the tenant's existing due-diligence rigor (i.e., must not make it easier to accidentally over-represent controls at scale, faster than a human alone would have).

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** No new framework — extend the existing hand-rolled stack (`anthropic` SDK direct via `backend/ai_service.py`/`ai_providers.py`, `chromadb` via `backend/rag_service.py`, Pydantic output validation per `backend/compliance_narrative_service.py`'s established pattern)

**Version:** `anthropic>=0.28.0`, `chromadb>=0.5.0` (both already pinned in `backend/requirements.txt` — no version change)

**Rationale:**
This codebase already has every primitive RAG-01/RAG-02 need, proven in production across Phases 12/13/15: `rag_service.py` (ChromaDB `PersistentClient`, `ingest_text()`/`query()`) for retrieval, `ai_service.generate_text()` (multi-provider, guardrails via `guardrail_service.py`, circuit breaker) for generation, `compliance_narrative_service.py`'s "Pydantic-validated output + static-template fallback" pattern for structured, safe generation, and the Phase 15 evidence-review workflow's submit/approve/reject/needs-revision shape for the mandatory human-in-the-loop gate RAG-02 requires. Introducing LangChain/LlamaIndex/CrewAI would add abstraction this codebase does not otherwise carry and would contradict the "don't hand-roll, don't over-engineer" discipline established across every phase this milestone.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LlamaIndex | Only justified if ChromaDB's default embedding + simple `query()` proves insufficient for multi-format questionnaire/evidence retrieval (reranking, hybrid search, table-heavy PDF parsing) — not justified at current scope; revisit if retrieval quality becomes a bottleneck |
| LangChain / CrewAI / LangGraph | Not present anywhere in `backend/requirements.txt`; this codebase's established pattern (Phases 12/13) is direct SDK calls with hand-rolled structured output, not an agent-orchestration framework — introducing one now for a single RAG+review flow would be net-new abstraction with no reuse benefit |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial — already accepted project-wide via `anthropic` as primary provider, but `ai_providers.py`'s multi-provider abstraction (Gemini/Ollama fallback) keeps this phase model-agnostic, consistent with the existing codebase posture, not a new decision.

```python
class AnswerDraft(BaseModel):
    question_id: str
    answer_text: str
    confidence: str  # "high" | "medium" | "low" | "insufficient_evidence"
    source_evidence_ids: list[str]
    word_count: int

    @field_validator("answer_text")
    @classmethod
    def not_empty_or_error_string(cls, v: str) -> str:
        v = v.strip()
        if not v:
            raise ValueError("answer_text must not be empty")
        if v.startswith(("BLOCKED:", "Error:")):
            raise ValueError(f"LLM returned error string: {v[:80]}")
        return v

    @model_validator(mode="after")
    def grounded_or_flagged(self) -> "AnswerDraft":
        # Critical Failure Mode #1/#4: a confident answer with zero cited
        # evidence is exactly the hallucination shape this phase must reject.
        if not self.source_evidence_ids and self.confidence != "insufficient_evidence":
            raise ValueError(
                "answer_text has no source_evidence_ids but confidence is not "
                "'insufficient_evidence' — every claim must cite retrieved evidence"
            )
        return self


async def draft_answer_for_question(
    question_id: str, question_text: str, tenant_id: str, db,
) -> dict:
    """Retrieve tenant evidence, draft a grounded answer, hold for human review.

    Clones compliance_narrative_service.py's "Pydantic-validated output +
    fallback" pattern for generation, and evidence_review_service.py's
    "pending_review" gate for the mandatory human-in-the-loop step (RAG-02).
    """
    set_tenant_id(tenant_id)  # guardrail_service logs against this ContextVar

    # 1. Retrieve. CRITICAL GAP — see Pitfall #1: rag_service.query() has no
    #    tenant filter today. Do not ship this call unmodified.
    retrieved = rag_service.query(question_text, n_results=5)
    if not retrieved:
        return _insufficient_evidence_draft(question_id, tenant_id)

    evidence_block = "\n".join(
        f"[{i}] (source: {e['source']}) {e['content']}" for i, e in enumerate(retrieved)
    )
    system_part = (
        "You are a security questionnaire response assistant. Answer using ONLY "
        "the evidence provided below. Cite the evidence indices you used. If the "
        "evidence does not support a confident answer, say so explicitly — do not "
        "invent claims. Respond as raw JSON: {\"answer_text\": str, "
        "\"confidence\": \"high\"|\"medium\"|\"low\"|\"insufficient_evidence\", "
        "\"cited_indices\": [int]}"
    )
    user_part = f"<evidence>\n{evidence_block}\n</evidence>\n\nQUESTION: {question_text}"

    raw = await ai_service.generate_text(
        f"{system_part}\n\n{user_part}", source="questionnaire_auto_answer"
    )
    if raw.startswith(("BLOCKED:", "Error:")):
        return _insufficient_evidence_draft(question_id, tenant_id, reason=raw[:120])

    try:
        parsed = json.loads(raw.replace("```json", "").replace("```", "").strip())
        cited = [retrieved[i]["source"] for i in parsed.get("cited_indices", []) if i < len(retrieved)]
        draft = AnswerDraft(
            question_id=question_id,
            answer_text=parsed["answer_text"],
            confidence=parsed.get("confidence", "low"),
            source_evidence_ids=cited,
            word_count=len(parsed["answer_text"].split()),
        )
    except (ValidationError, KeyError, json.JSONDecodeError) as exc:
        logger.warning("[QuestionnaireAutoAnswer] validation failed: %s", exc)
        return _insufficient_evidence_draft(question_id, tenant_id, reason=str(exc)[:120])

    # 2. Hold for human review — never auto-submit (Critical Failure Mode #2).
    doc = {
        "id": f"qad-{uuid.uuid4().hex}",
        "tenantId": tenant_id,
        "questionId": question_id,
        "answerText": draft.answer_text,
        "confidence": draft.confidence,
        "sourceEvidenceIds": draft.source_evidence_ids,
        "status": "pending_review",  # mirrors evidence_review_service.py's gate
        "created_at": datetime.now(timezone.utc).isoformat(),
    }
    await db.questionnaire_answer_drafts.insert_one(doc)
    return doc
```

### Common Pitfalls
1. **`rag_service.py`'s ChromaDB collection is NOT tenant-scoped today.** `self.collection = self.client.get_or_create_collection(name="omni-agent-knowledge")` is one global collection; `ingest_text()` writes no `tenantId` into metadata (confirmed by the only current caller, `ingest_security_standards.py:50`), and `query()` passes no `where` filter. Calling `rag_service.query()` unmodified for this phase returns evidence from every tenant, directly causing Critical Failure Mode #3 (cross-tenant leak). **This must be fixed before Phase 30 ships** — either partition by collection name (`f"tenant-{tenant_id}-evidence"`) or add `"tenantId": tenant_id` to `ingest_text()`'s metadata dict and pass `where={"tenantId": tenant_id}` into `collection.query()`. Flag this to `gsd-planner` as a required prerequisite task on `rag_service.py` itself, not something to work around at the call site.
2. **`ai_service.generate_text()` exposes no `temperature` or `max_tokens` parameter.** `AnthropicProvider.generate()` hardcodes `"max_tokens": 1024` (`ai_providers.py:172`) and omits `temperature` entirely (provider default ≈ 1.0). Grounded, low-creativity questionnaire answers need low temperature (0–0.2) and headroom above 1024 output tokens for longer justifications with citations — neither is currently reachable through `generate_text()`. Either extend `generate_text()`/`AIProvider.generate()` to accept `temperature`/`max_tokens` kwargs, or call `AsyncAnthropic` directly the way `agentic_service.py` does (losing `ai_service`'s guardrail/circuit-breaker wrapping — an explicit trade-off, not a free win).
3. **`generate_text()` takes one flattened prompt string — no native system/user separation.** Unlike `agentic_service.py`'s direct `AsyncAnthropic` client (which passes `system=`), `ai_service.generate_text()` has a single `prompt` argument. `compliance_narrative_service.py`'s established workaround — concatenating `f"{system_part}\n\n{user_part}"` — is weaker instruction-following than a true system message but is the codebase convention; follow it for consistency rather than introducing a new pattern for this one phase.
4. **The `str.replace("```json","").replace("```","").strip()` → `json.loads()` pattern used everywhere (`analyze_impact`, `negotiate_agent_action`) is brittle** and throws `json.JSONDecodeError` on any prose-wrapped or malformed response. Always wrap in `try/except (json.JSONDecodeError, KeyError)` and route straight to the insufficient-evidence fallback — never let a parse failure bubble up as a 500, and never treat a parse failure as "no evidence" silently (log it, per Critical Failure Mode #4).
5. **A `generate_text()` return value does not visibly distinguish "guardrail-blocked" from "genuinely low-quality answer."** Both are ordinary strings. Always check `result.startswith(("BLOCKED:", "Error:"))` before treating output as usable — exactly as `compliance_narrative_service.py` does at every call site. Skipping this check risks writing a guardrail-block message into `answerText` as though it were a real drafted answer, which a distracted reviewer could approve.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**
Primary: Anthropic `claude-sonnet-4-6` (`AnthropicProvider`, `ai_providers.py:145`), selected automatically by `IncidentAnalyzer.initialize()`'s fallback chain when `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is set (`ai_service.py:132-137`) — the same model already used by `agentic_service.py`'s structured-decision calls (`ai_providers.py:299`, `:365`). `max_tokens` is hardcoded to `1024` in `AnthropicProvider.generate()` (`ai_providers.py:172`) and `temperature` is not passed at all (see Pitfall #2) — this phase needs both raised/lowered respectively (`max_tokens` ~1536-2048, `temperature` 0-0.2) and neither is currently plumbed through `generate_text()`; extending that signature is in-scope implementation work for this phase, not optional polish. Multi-provider fallback (Gemini → Ollama → MockProvider) is inherited automatically via `ai_service.initialize()` — no phase-specific provider config needed, consistent with Section 2's "model-agnostic" framework decision.

**Core Pattern:**
Retrieve → grounded-generate → Pydantic-validate → hold-for-review, cloned directly from two existing services rather than invented fresh:
- Retrieval + generation + validation: `compliance_narrative_service.generate_framework_narrative()`'s shape — sanitize inputs, build a system/user prompt around a fenced `<data>` block, call `ai_service.generate_text()`, check the `BLOCKED:`/`Error:` prefix, then validate via Pydantic with an explicit fallback on `ValidationError` (see the `AnswerDraft` model and `draft_answer_for_question()` entry point above).
- Human-in-the-loop gate: `evidence_review_service.py`'s `submit_for_review()` / `create_review()` / `update_review_decision()` triad — atomic status-scoped updates, dedup-upsert to prevent duplicate review threads on the same draft, and a re-check of status at decision-write time. The questionnaire-answer review endpoint should be a near-verbatim clone of this file with `evidence_id → draft_id` and the `asset_compliance` collection swapped for `questionnaire_answer_drafts`.

**Tool Use:**
No `tools=`/function-calling is needed for the core draft path — this is single-turn RAG + generation, not agentic tool selection (contrast with `agentic_service.py`'s two-turn `tool_choice={"type": "any"}` loop, which does not apply here). `rag_service.query()` is the sole "retrieval tool," invoked directly in application code rather than exposed to the model as an Anthropic tool schema.

**State Management:**
New MongoDB collection `questionnaire_answer_drafts`, tenant-scoped via a `tenantId` field following every existing collection's convention (`questionnaires`, `asset_compliance`, `evidence_reviews`). Status vocabulary mirrors `evidence_review_service.py`: `pending_review → approved | rejected | needs_revision`. No path to "submitted" may exist while `status != "approved"` — enforce this as a DB-query-level guard (e.g. `{"status": "approved"}` in the filter of whatever "mark submitted" endpoint is built), not merely a UI-level check, to close Critical Failure Mode #2 at the data layer.

**Context Window Strategy:**
`rag_service.query(question_text, n_results=5)` (default is `n_results=3`; raise to 5 here since a questionnaire answer needs more supporting detail than a chat reply) bounds retrieved context to a small, fixed chunk count — but `rag_service.py` has no token-budget check today (unlike `agentic_service.py`'s `estimate_tokens()`/`truncate_security_context()`, `agentic_service.py:247-277`). If ingested evidence documents turn out to be long, adopt that same `estimate_tokens()` pattern (`len(json.dumps(...)) // 4`) before assembling the prompt, and truncate per-chunk content (e.g. to ~500 words each) rather than dropping whole chunks, so no single retrieved document silently pushes the prompt over the model's context window.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

### Structured Outputs with Pydantic

`AnswerDraft` (defined in Section 3's Entry Point Pattern) is the structured-output contract, validated the same way `compliance_narrative_service.NarrativeOutput` validates narrative text: parse the LLM's JSON, construct the Pydantic model, and catch `ValidationError` at the call site — never let it propagate to the API layer.

```python
class AnswerDraft(BaseModel):
    question_id: str
    answer_text: str
    confidence: str  # "high" | "medium" | "low" | "insufficient_evidence"
    source_evidence_ids: list[str]
    word_count: int

    @field_validator("answer_text")
    @classmethod
    def not_empty_or_error_string(cls, v: str) -> str:
        v = v.strip()
        if not v:
            raise ValueError("answer_text must not be empty")
        if v.startswith(("BLOCKED:", "Error:")):
            raise ValueError(f"LLM returned error string: {v[:80]}")
        return v

    @model_validator(mode="after")
    def grounded_or_flagged(self) -> "AnswerDraft":
        if not self.source_evidence_ids and self.confidence != "insufficient_evidence":
            raise ValueError(
                "answer_text has no source_evidence_ids but confidence is not "
                "'insufficient_evidence' — every claim must cite retrieved evidence"
            )
        return self
```

**Retry logic:** `compliance_narrative_service.py` does **not** retry on `ValidationError` — it falls back immediately to a static template (`_fallback_executive_summary`). This phase should follow the same immediate-fallback discipline, but with one deliberate difference: there is no safe static *answer* for a security questionnaire (unlike an executive summary, a wrong boilerplate compliance claim is itself a risk), so the fallback on any validation failure is `_insufficient_evidence_draft(...)` — a record with `confidence: "insufficient_evidence"` and empty `answer_text` surfaced to the human reviewer as "needs manual drafting," never a plausible-sounding placeholder. Log every validation failure at `WARNING` with the raw LLM output truncated to ~120 chars (matches `compliance_narrative_service.py`'s `exc` logging convention). Zero retries on the generation call itself is correct here: `ai_service.generate_text()` already retries transient provider failures internally (`_retries=3`, exponential backoff, `ai_service.py:209-294`) — a second retry loop on top of that for validation failures would only mask a genuinely malformed model response, which is exactly the case that must surface to a human, not be silently re-tried away.

### Async-First Design

Every service function in this phase is `async def`, matching the codebase's 100% convention. One nuance to carry forward: `rag_service.query()` and `rag_service.ingest_text()` are **not** async — they are synchronous `chromadb` calls wrapped in plain `def` methods (`rag_service.py:28`, `:55`). Call them from an `async def` function without `await`; at the low per-questionnaire volumes here (dozens of questions) blocking the event loop briefly is acceptable, but if this becomes a bottleneck, wrap the call in `asyncio.to_thread(rag_service.query, question_text, 5)` rather than leaving a synchronous ChromaDB call inline in a hot async path. The one common mistake to avoid: never call `asyncio.run()` inside `draft_answer_for_question()` or any sibling service function — it already executes inside FastAPI's existing event loop, reached from an `async def` endpoint handler, exactly as `ai_service.generate_text()` itself demonstrates (`ai_service.py:213` imports `asyncio` only for `_asyncio.sleep()` in its backoff loop, never `.run()`). Stream vs. await: use `await ai_service.generate_text(...)`, not `chat_stream()`/`generate_stream()` — this is a structured-output path that must be fully Pydantic-validated before it is usable at all, so token-by-token streaming (as `chat_stream()` does for the assistant chat UI) provides no UX benefit here and only complicates validation of a response that isn't complete yet.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

System vs. user: `generate_text()` takes one flattened string, so follow `compliance_narrative_service.py`'s exact convention — concatenate a static `system_part` (persona + hard constraint: "answer using ONLY the evidence provided... do not invent claims") with a per-call `user_part` built from sanitized, fenced `<evidence>...</evidence>` and the raw `QUESTION:` text. Sanitize every field pulled from evidence metadata (particularly `source`) with a `compliance_narrative_service._sanitise()`-equivalent step (strip `<>{}[]\`, collapse embedded newlines) before it enters the prompt — this is a RAG-specific injection surface distinct from user-input injection: a prompt-injection payload can live *inside ingested evidence documents themselves*, not just in the live user request, so both the evidence content and its `source` label must be treated as untrusted. Few-shot: none is used anywhere in this codebase today (`compliance_narrative_service.py` and `agentic_service.py` are both zero-shot with strict output-format instructions in the system prompt) — stay consistent; add few-shot examples only if the eval dataset (Section 5) shows the model drifting off the required JSON shape. `max_tokens`: once `generate_text()` is extended per Pitfall #2, set it explicitly on every call (recommend 1536-2048 for questionnaire answers with citations) — never leave it at whatever the provider defaults to in production, since an unbounded/under-specified ceiling risks either truncating a grounded answer mid-citation or (with no cap at all) an unexpectedly expensive response.

### Context Window Management

RAG-specific for this system type: `rag_service.query(question_text, n_results=5)` already bounds the number of retrieved chunks, but has no token-budget check on their combined size (`rag_service.py` has nothing equivalent to `agentic_service.truncate_security_context()`). Add that same `estimate_tokens()` pattern (`len(json.dumps(...)) // 4`, `agentic_service.py:247-249`) before assembling the evidence block, truncating any single evidence chunk to ~500 words rather than allowing 5 chunks of unbounded length to blow the context window. Because this is also a Structured Extraction system, keep the fast-path short-circuit shown in the Entry Point Pattern: `if not retrieved: return _insufficient_evidence_draft(...)` — an empty retrieval result must never reach the model at all, both to save the call and because Critical Failure Mode #4 requires no-evidence cases to be flagged, not drafted around.

### Cost and Latency Budget

Model: `claude-sonnet-4-6`, roughly $3/$15 per million input/output tokens at time of writing (verify current Anthropic rate card before finalizing a budget). Per-question estimate: ~5 evidence chunks × ~150 words + question + system prompt ≈ 1,200-1,800 input tokens, ~300-500 output tokens (answer text + citation indices) ≈ $0.01-0.015 per question at current rates; a 50-question inbound questionnaire ≈ $0.50-0.75 to fully draft. Caching: no caching layer exists in `ai_service.py` today — every `generate_text()` call is a fresh provider round-trip. Vendor security questionnaires frequently reuse boilerplate SOC2/ISO27001 question sets across submissions, so an exact-match cache keyed on `(tenant_id, normalized_question_text)` is a reasonable first step before investing in full semantic caching; this does not exist yet and should be an explicit, scoped decision for `gsd-planner` rather than an assumption baked silently into the implementation. Sub-task model routing: this phase has exactly one LLM call type (drafting) — there is no classification/routing sub-task to offload to a cheaper model, unlike `agentic_service.py`'s two-turn tool-selection pattern. If a future phase adds question-type pre-classification (e.g., yes/no vs. free-text vs. file-upload) ahead of drafting, that step is a good candidate for a cheaper/faster model per the general principle — but it is out of scope for RAG-01/RAG-02 as currently defined.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

> Dimensions are grounded directly in Section 1b's five domain rubric ingredients (evidence traceability, certainty calibration, scope precision, insufficient-evidence honesty, reviewability), not generic RAG metrics. `AnswerDraft`'s Pydantic validators (Section 3/4b) enforce the code-checkable floor of several of these at write time; the eval layer measures what validation cannot — semantic grounding, overclaiming, and scope match — on top of that floor.

### Dimensions

| Dimension | Rubric (Pass/Fail) | Measurement Approach | Priority |
|-----------|--------------------|----------------------|----------|
| **Evidence traceability (grounding)** | PASS: every factual claim in `answer_text` is supported by the specific content of at least one cited `source_evidence_ids` entry, verifiable by a reviewer clicking through. FAIL: a claim in the text has no cited evidence supporting it, or the cited evidence is real but does not actually support that specific claim (e.g., cites a firewall policy doc to support an encryption-at-rest claim). | Code (structural: `AnswerDraft.grounded_or_flagged` — non-empty text requires non-empty `source_evidence_ids`) + LLM Judge (semantic: does cited evidence *actually* support the claim, RAGAS `faithfulness`) | Critical |
| **Certainty calibration (no overclaiming)** | PASS: answer states current/verified practice only where evidence shows it is current, and explicitly flags partial/planned/in-progress controls as such (e.g., "in progress," "partial rollout"). FAIL: answer asserts a control is fully implemented when cited evidence shows only partial, planned, or stale coverage — optimistic drift per Section 1b's "most common real-world failure" finding. | LLM Judge (rubric: compare tense/certainty language in `answer_text` against the qualifying language in the cited evidence chunks) + Human (calibration sample) | Critical |
| **Scope and boundary precision** | PASS: answer's stated scope (product, environment, data classification, region) matches both the cited evidence's actual scope and the inbound question's scope. FAIL: evidence scoped to one environment (e.g., prod AWS only) is presented as tenant-wide, or a scoped question gets an unscoped blanket answer. | LLM Judge (rubric: extract scope terms from question, evidence, and answer; flag mismatch) | High |
| **Insufficient-evidence honesty (no silent gap-filling)** | PASS: when retrieval returns nothing on-topic (or the model determines no chunk supports a confident answer), `confidence == "insufficient_evidence"` and `answer_text` is empty/flagged — never generic best-practice-sounding boilerplate presented as tenant-specific. FAIL: a no-match or weak-match case produces confident, plausible-sounding text that isn't actually grounded in this tenant's real controls. | Code (`AnswerDraft.grounded_or_flagged` catches the zero-evidence + non-`insufficient_evidence` combination structurally) + LLM Judge (catches the harder case: evidence *was* retrieved but is topically weak/tangential, yet the model answered confidently anyway) | Critical |
| **Reviewability of draft-to-approval trail** | PASS: every record that reaches `approved`/`submitted` has a retrievable original draft, cited evidence list, reviewer identity, decision timestamp, and a diff-able record of any edit between drafted and approved text. FAIL: a record reaches `approved` with no reviewer identity, no draft/final diff, or no way to reconstruct whether a human actually read it. | Code (schema/DB-level: required fields present on every `approved` record — `reviewer_id`, `decided_at`, `original_answer_text` vs. `answer_text` diff) | High |
| **Tenant isolation (retrieval scope, security)** | PASS: `rag_service.query()` for tenant A returns zero chunks originating from any other tenant's ingested evidence, verified by cross-tenant fixture test. FAIL: any chunk in tenant A's retrieved evidence set traces back to a different `tenantId`. | Code (deterministic integration test — see Section 6 guardrail; this is Critical Failure Mode #3 and must be closed as a prerequisite, not merely evaluated) | Critical |
| **Human-approval gate integrity (task completion / safety)** | PASS: 100% of records with `status == "submitted"` have a preceding `status == "approved"` transition with a `reviewer_id`. FAIL: any `submitted` record exists whose status history does not include a prior `approved` transition, or was written by a code path that bypasses the review service. | Code (DB-query-level guard per Section 4, exercised by an automated regression test that attempts direct `pending_review` → `submitted` and asserts rejection) | Critical |

### Eval Tooling

No eval/tracing tooling (Langfuse, LangSmith, Arize Phoenix, RAGAS, Promptfoo) is present anywhere in `backend/requirements.txt` or the codebase today (confirmed by repo-wide scan) — this phase establishes the first eval tooling for the platform's AI surfaces, not just this feature. Given Section 2's "no new framework, hand-rolled stack" decision, keep the eval tooling itself equally lightweight: no hosted platform account, CLI/library-only, consistent with the codebase's existing discipline.

### Reference Dataset

**Size:** 15 examples to start (within the 10-20 range `ai-evals.md` recommends) — small enough for the Product owner / GRC Manager stand-in (Section 1b) to hand-label directly, expand from real reviewer disagreements once in production.

**Composition:**
- 5 critical-path examples: question with clear, current, well-scoped evidence available (the "should draft confidently and correctly" case)
- 3 optimistic-drift traps: evidence shows a partial/planned control; gold answer must hedge, a naive model is likely to overclaim (directly targets Section 1b's #1 documented failure pattern)
- 2 scope-mismatch traps: evidence scoped to one environment/product only; question is broader or narrower than the evidence's actual coverage
- 2 zero-evidence cases: question has no matching ingested evidence at all — gold answer is `insufficient_evidence`
- 1 stale-evidence case: matching evidence exists but is old enough to trigger the platform's existing `STALE-01` threshold — gold answer must flag staleness, not cite it as current
- 2 cross-tenant adversarial cases: evidence from a second seeded tenant exists in the same ChromaDB instance; gold expectation is zero leakage into tenant A's retrieved set (feeds the tenant-isolation code test, not the LLM judge)

**Labeling:** Per Section 1b's "Domain Expert Roles" table — the Product owner (internal stand-in until a dedicated compliance hire/design partner is available) hand-labels all 15 gold answers plus expected `confidence`/`source_evidence_ids` during Verify phase, then calibrates the LLM judge prompt against their own labels (target ≥0.7 agreement per `ai-evals.md` before trusting the judge un-sampled). Dataset construction starts during implementation (alongside `questionnaire_answer_draft_service.py`), not after — per `ai-evals.md`'s Execute-phase guidance.

## 6. Guardrails

> Per `ai-evals.md`'s guardrail-vs-flywheel test ("would this be catastrophic if wrong?") — three of Section 1's four Critical Failure Modes are catastrophic-if-wrong (contractual misrepresentation, bypassed approval, cross-tenant data leak) and are therefore online guardrails, not sampled quality signals.

### Online (Real-Time)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| **Mandatory human-review gate (RAG-02 hard guardrail)** | Any request to transition a `questionnaire_answer_drafts` record to `submitted` status | **Block** at the DB-query level — the "mark submitted" endpoint's update filter must include `{"status": "approved"}`; a record in `pending_review`, `rejected`, or `needs_revision` cannot match that filter and the transition fails closed, not merely a UI-disabled button (Section 4 already specifies this at the data layer — this guardrail exists to make sure it is *tested*, not just implemented: a regression test asserting a direct `pending_review → submitted` API/DB call is rejected must exist and run in CI, not be assumed from the code review alone) |
| **Tenant-scoped retrieval (cross-tenant leak prevention)** | Every `rag_service.query()` call made from `draft_answer_for_question()` | **Block/Escalate** — `rag_service.py`'s ChromaDB collection must be tenant-scoped (per Section 3 Pitfall #1: either per-tenant collection naming or a `where={"tenantId": tenant_id}` filter on `ingest_text()`/`query()`) **before this phase ships**; this is a required prerequisite fix to `rag_service.py` itself, not a Phase 30 call-site workaround. Treat the fix as unverified until a dedicated tenant-isolation test exists and passes: seed evidence for two distinct tenants into the same ChromaDB instance, query as tenant A, assert zero chunks carry tenant B's `tenantId`. This test must run in CI (`test_rag_service_tenant_isolation.py`) and gate merge of both the `rag_service.py` fix and any code that calls it from this phase. |
| **Structural grounding check (empty-evidence guardrail)** | Every `AnswerDraft` construction inside `draft_answer_for_question()` | **Flag → fallback** — `AnswerDraft.grounded_or_flagged` model validator already rejects (raises `ValidationError`) any draft with a non-`insufficient_evidence` confidence and zero `source_evidence_ids`; on that `ValidationError`, the service must route to `_insufficient_evidence_draft(...)` per Section 4b, never let the exception surface as a 500 or silently drop the question |
| **Blocked/error-string leak into `answerText`** | Every `ai_service.generate_text()` return value inside `draft_answer_for_question()` | **Block** — check `raw.startswith(("BLOCKED:", "Error:"))` before any parsing/Pydantic construction (Section 3 Pitfall #5); on match, route to `_insufficient_evidence_draft(...)` with the block/error reason logged, never let a guardrail-block or provider-error string reach a reviewer disguised as a real drafted answer |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Human-edit rate (draft vs. approved text diff)** | 100% of `approved` records (not sampled — cheap to compute, high signal; this is Section 1's `eval_concerns` flag). Compute a normalized edit distance between `original_answer_text` (drafted) and final `answer_text` (as approved) on every decision. | Sustained high edit-rate on a specific question category or evidence source signals a systematic drafting weakness (e.g., consistently overclaiming for a given control type) — feeds prompt/evidence-retrieval refinement, tracked per Section 1b's rubric dimensions so edits can be tagged by *which* dimension the reviewer corrected (grounding / calibration / scope) |
| **Optimistic-drift rate** | Weighted sample: all records where cited evidence's `confidence`/staleness metadata suggests partial or planned coverage (favor these over uniform random sampling, per `ai-evals.md`'s "smart sampling" guidance) | LLM-judge re-score against the certainty-calibration rubric monthly; rising drift rate triggers a prompt-language review of the system prompt's hedging instructions |
| **Insufficient-evidence rate by question category** | 100% of `insufficient_evidence` records, grouped by question topic/keyword | A rising rate for a specific control area signals an evidence *ingestion* gap (not a drafting gap) — routes to the compliance team as a "go collect this evidence" work item, not a prompt fix |
| **Reviewer approval latency / rubber-stamp risk** | Sample records approved with near-zero elapsed time between draft creation and approval decision, weighted toward long questionnaires (100+ questions) per Section 1b's documented reviewer-fatigue pattern | Flag for Compliance Manager spot-check; if a pattern of sub-30-second approvals on multi-paragraph answers emerges, escalate to a UX fix (surface evidence more prominently) rather than a model fix |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** Arize Phoenix, self-hosted (open-source, OpenTelemetry-based) — chosen because no tracing/observability tool exists anywhere in this codebase today (confirmed by repo scan) and Phoenix requires no new vendor account or SaaS dependency, consistent with Section 2's "don't hand-roll, don't over-engineer, but don't add unnecessary vendor lock-in either" posture. Instrument at the two call sites already identified in Section 3: `rag_service.query()` (retrieval span — record `tenant_id`, `n_results`, retrieved `source` list) and `ai_service.generate_text()` (generation span — record `source="questionnaire_auto_answer"`, token counts, `BLOCKED:`/`Error:` outcome). If this platform later adopts LangSmith/Langfuse platform-wide, migrate then — do not pre-adopt a platform for a single phase.

**Key Metrics to Track:**
1. **Human-edit rate** (draft-to-approved text diff, per Section 6 flywheel) — the single most direct signal of drafting quality since it reflects actual reviewer correction behavior, not a proxy judge score
2. **Insufficient-evidence rate** (% of questions where retrieval/generation produces `confidence == "insufficient_evidence"`) — tracks both drafting honesty and evidence-ingestion coverage gaps
3. **Approval-gate integrity** (count of any `submitted` record found without a preceding `approved` transition — should always be exactly zero; any non-zero value is a Sev-1 incident, not a metric to trend)
4. **Cross-tenant retrieval violations** (count of any retrieved chunk in a trace whose `tenantId` metadata does not match the requesting `tenant_id` — should always be exactly zero post-fix; wire as a Phoenix span assertion, not just a batch check)
5. **Cost/latency per drafted answer** (input/output token counts and wall-clock time per `generate_text()` call, per Section 4b's $0.01-0.015/question budget) — drift beyond ~2x the documented estimate signals either a prompt/context-window regression or a pricing change worth re-budgeting

**Alert Thresholds:**
- Approval-gate integrity metric > 0 → page immediately (Sev-1; this is Critical Failure Mode #2 realized in production, a compliance-control failure, not a quality issue)
- Cross-tenant retrieval violation metric > 0 → page immediately (Sev-1; Critical Failure Mode #3 realized in production, a security incident)
- Human-edit rate exceeding a rolling 30-day baseline by >25% for a given question category → non-paging alert to the GRC/Compliance owner for prompt review
- Insufficient-evidence rate exceeding 20% of a given questionnaire's total questions → non-paging alert (likely an evidence-ingestion gap for that tenant, route to Customer Success/Compliance, not engineering)
- Cost per drafted answer exceeding 2x the Section 4b budget estimate sustained over 24h → non-paging alert to engineering (context-window or prompt regression)

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**
Per `ai-evals.md`'s "weight toward interactions with concerning signals" guidance, apply signal-based sampling rather than uniform random review, in this priority order:
1. **Every** `approved` record where the human-edit distance exceeds a fixed threshold (large diff between draft and final = high-value calibration signal for the LLM judge and the prompt)
2. **Every** record flagged `insufficient_evidence` (100% sample — cheap, and directly measures whether Critical Failure Mode #4 is actually being avoided in production, not just in the reference dataset)
3. Weighted sample of `low`/`medium` confidence approvals (these are the likeliest home for undetected optimistic-drift per Section 1b's #1 documented failure pattern — oversample relative to `high`-confidence approvals)
4. Random 5% baseline sample of all remaining `approved` records, to catch signal-metric divergence (per `ai-evals.md`) — cases where the code/LLM-judge metrics look fine but a human reviewer would flag a problem the automated checks missed
